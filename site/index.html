Cluster headache · patient groups

You don't have to go through this alone

Patient groups and trusted information about cluster headache in your own language. If you have attacks but no diagnosis, or you can't get treatment that works, start with the box below.

Start here

clusterinfo.org explains what stops attacks and what prevents them. It has:

  • A 2-minute self-check (it is not a diagnosis)
  • How cluster headache differs from migraine
  • A short quiz that makes a summary you can take to your doctor
  • Guides to high-flow oxygen, verapamil and vitamin D3
Go to clusterinfo.org Treatment access: clusterfree.org

Both sites are in English. Chrome, Edge and Safari can translate them for you. Take what you learn to your GP or neurologist rather than treating yourself. Some guides cover substances that are illegal in many countries.

Does this sound like you?

  • Very severe pain on one side, around or behind the eye or temple
  • Attacks last 15 minutes to 3 hours, often at the same time of day or night
  • A red or watering eye, a blocked or runny nose, or a drooping eyelid on the same side
  • You can't lie still during an attack

These are the main features doctors look for. Only a doctor can diagnose it. Many people wait years for the right diagnosis, so ask about cluster headache by name.
